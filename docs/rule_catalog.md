# COMMFACT Rule Catalog (Tier 1 – MVP)

> **Purpose**  
This document lists the governance rules implemented in COMMFACT Lite, including their normative regulatory basis.  
It is designed for **agency transparency, audit discussion, and pilot use**.  

> **Important note**  
This catalog provides **normative alignment**, not legal opinions.  
Rules function as **risk indicators**, and **final decisions remain with human reviewers**.

---

## LGL-ABS-01 — Absolute Claim
**Severity:** HIGH

### Rule Description
Statements that present outcomes, conditions, or safety as **absolute or unconditional**, without reasonable qualification.

### Indicative Linguistic Examples
- "100% aman"
- "selalu berhasil"
- "tanpa risiko sama sekali"
- "pasti aman untuk semua"

> These examples are **indicative**, not exhaustive.

### Normative Regulatory Basis (Indonesia)
- **BPOM – Pedoman Periklanan Produk Kesehatan**  
  Advertising must not use absolute or unconditional claims that may mislead consumers.

- **Etika Pariwara Indonesia (EPI)**  
  Advertisements should avoid wording that removes reasonable doubt or critical judgement from the audience.

- **Kominfo – Principles of Responsible Public Information**  
  Public information should not create unrealistic certainty or negate audience caution.

### Governance Rationale
Absolute claims reduce audience critical capacity and increase reputational and regulatory exposure.  
All detected instances **must be reviewed by a human decision-maker**.

## LGL-SUP-01 — Superiority Claim
**Severity:** MEDIUM–HIGH

### Rule Description
Statements asserting **comparative or superlative superiority** without explicit, verifiable reference.

### Indicative Linguistic Examples
- "terbaik"
- "nomor satu"
- "paling direkomendasikan"
- "lebih unggul dari semua"

> Context and substantiation determine final assessment.

### Normative Regulatory Basis (Indonesia)
- **Etika Pariwara Indonesia (EPI)**  
  Comparative and superlative claims must be factually substantiated and clearly referenced.

- **KPI – P3SPS (Broadcasting Standards)**  
  Broadcast content must not manipulate public perception through unsupported dominance claims.

- **Kominfo – Digital Content Guidelines**  
  Digital commercial content should avoid misleading implications of market dominance or superiority.

### Governance Rationale
Superiority claims are a frequent source of competitive dispute and reputational challenge.  
They require **explicit justification during review**, even when linguistically subtle.

## LGL-GUA-01 — Guarantee Claim
**Severity:** HIGH

### Rule Description
Statements that **guarantee outcomes, results, or success**, particularly in health, performance, or impact claims.

### Indicative Linguistic Examples
- "dijamin berhasil"
- "pasti sembuh"
- "100% efektif"
- "tanpa kemungkinan gagal"

> Guarantee wording represents the **highest governance risk**.

### Normative Regulatory Basis (Indonesia)
- **BPOM – Promotion and Advertising Guidelines**  
  Prohibits guarantees of medical outcomes, effectiveness, or recovery.

- **Kementerian Kesehatan – Health Communication Ethics**  
  Health-related information must avoid creating excessive or unrealistic expectations.

- **Consumer Protection Principles (General)**  
  Guaranteed outcome claims may constitute misleading information and increase liability exposure.

### Governance Rationale
Guarantee claims are among the most common triggers of regulatory action and public backlash.  
Content flagged under this rule **cannot be auto-approved** under COMMFACT governance.

## General Notes on Rule Interpretation

- Linguistic indicators function as **risk triggers**, not automatic violations.
- Absence of listed keywords does **not** imply content safety.
- Rule evaluation always requires **human judgement and documented reasoning**.
